## WINERY PROGRAM PAGE — Shot List (6 Directives)

*Mood reference: Dark, contemplative, documentary. Think National Geographic meets a fine art wine book.
Editing: Rich shadows, deep blacks, warm mid-tones. High contrast but never harsh.*

---

1. **The Approach — Exterior Arrival**
Wide shot of the Bodega estate approach — a long gravel path flanked by low vines, leading toward a whitewashed stone building. Late morning light, slightly hazy. One solitary figure (a guest or the sommelier) walking away from camera toward the entrance. No direct eye contact. The scale of the frame should make the person feel appropriately small against the landscape — reverent, not diminished.

2. **The Cellar Entrance — Threshold Moment**
The sommelier standing in the doorway of the cellar — half in sunlight, half in shadow. Shot from inside the cellar looking out toward them. The contrast should be dramatic: bright Andalusian daylight behind them, the cool darkness of the cellar in the foreground. This is the single most important editorial image for this page. It must communicate: *you are entering something rare.*

3. **The Barrel Room — Patience**
A slow, wide interior shot of the barrel room. Rows of oak barrels receding into soft darkness. No people. A single shaft of light from a small ceiling vent cuts diagonally across the frame. Complete silence must be implied. The shot communicates aging, patience, and depth. Shot at f/2.8 or wider for maximum depth-of-field falloff.

4. **The Pour in Darkness**
The sommelier pouring wine in the cellar — only their hands and the glass visible in the frame, lit by a single candle or small side lamp. The wine appears luminous against the darkness. Shot from a 3/4 side angle. This is the wine workshop's signature hero image: intimate, almost ritualistic.

5. **The Student's Attention**
A small group of guests (3–4 people max visible in frame) listening intently to the sommelier in the cellar. One guest's notepad is visible — handwritten notes. Another holds a glass tilted at the correct 45° tasting angle. Nobody is smiling for the camera. This is concentration, not performance. Shot from slightly behind and to the side of the group — documentary style.

6. **The Vineyard — Terroir**
Extreme close-up of a single bunch of grapes on the vine — heavy, nearly overripe, with morning moisture still visible on the skin. The background is entirely blown out to soft gold. This image should feel abundant and sensual, not agricultural. Shot at macro distance, focus on a single grape within the cluster.

## ABOUT PAGE — Shot List (5 Directives)

*Mood reference: Intimate portraiture. The three founders/collaborators — sommelier, yoga teacher, chef — should each have their own visual language.
Editing: Warm, personal, slightly editorial. Less dramatic than the cellar shots — more human.*

---

1. **The Sommelier — Portrait**
The sommelier photographed in their natural habitat: holding a glass at eye level, examining the wine's clarity against natural window light. They are not looking at the camera — they are looking at the wine. The expression is one of quiet expertise, not showmanship. Shot at 85mm equivalent, wide aperture. The background should be softly identifiable as a cellar or wine storage space.

2. **The Yoga Teacher — Portrait**
The yoga teacher photographed at the beginning of the day, seated cross-legged on the terrace with the sea visible behind them. Their eyes are closed, hands resting on their knees. The light is the pale gold of early morning. The image must communicate serenity without cliché — no forced "enlightened" expression. Completely natural, almost stolen.

3. **The Chef — Portrait**
The chef photographed in the moment just before service — arranging a single plate with focused attention. Shot from directly above (overhead). Their hands are in motion, slightly blurred. The plate is immaculate. This portrait tells the story of the person through their craft, not their face.

4. **The Collaboration — The Three Together**
The three collaborators at the long outdoor table — a bottle of wine open, a plate of food half-eaten, conversation clearly mid-flow. Nobody is looking at the camera. Shot from a respectful distance with a longer lens to preserve the candid quality. This is the founding image of the brand: three people who built something together, still clearly enjoying it.

5. **The Space — Before the Guests Arrive**
An empty terrace — two yoga mats rolled out, a row of wine glasses set up on a stone ledge, a small ceramic bowl of olives. Shot in the very first light of the morning, before anyone has arrived. This is the "stage is set" image — and it must communicate that something intentional and beautiful is about to happen here. No people. Total stillness.
